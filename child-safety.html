Child Safety Standards – Viper Byte

Published by M.A.D.S. India · Last updated: 14 November 2026

Viper Byte is a retro-style Snake game that also contains a private, end-to-end encrypted chat between adults who have approved each other as "buddies". Viper Byte is made and run by one person, Debdyut Sarker ("the developer"); on this page "we" means the developer. This page explains our standards against child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE), and how anyone can reach us about a child safety concern.

Zero tolerance

  • Viper Byte has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and for any sexual abuse or exploitation of children. This includes grooming, sexual messages to a child, sextortion, and sharing, asking for or selling such material.
  • This is banned in our Terms of Use. Breaking it ends the account.

The chat is for adults only

  • The game can be played by anyone, but the chat is only for people aged 18 or over. Every user confirms they are 18 or over before the chat is set up.
  • There is no public chat, no groups and no way to search for strangers. Two people can talk only after both have approved each other.
  • The app is listed on Google Play for adults (18 and over).

Report and Block inside the app

  • On any buddy's page, choose Report and pick Child Safety (other reasons are Spam or Scam, Harassment or Threats, Sexual Content and Something Else).
  • You can Report and Block in one step. A blocked person can no longer reach you, and is not told.
  • A report sends us your private ID, their private ID, the reason and the time. The app then asks "Add Messages as Proof?" (Add Last 10 Messages or Without Messages; neither is picked for you). Reporting one message (press and hold it > Report) adds that message and the 9 before it.
  • Proof is the chat's last 10 messages as your phone shows them (photos made small; voice messages and view-once photos only noted). It is sealed on your phone so that only the developer can open it, on the developer's own computer. All other messages stay end-to-end encrypted: we cannot read them.
  • If a child is in danger right now, call 112 (emergency) or 1098 (CHILDLINE, India).

How we act on a report

  1. First in line: the developer looks at child safety reports personally, before any other report.
  2. Check the proof: the developer opens any proof added to the report. Proof cannot fully prove a message was really sent (a changed app could fake it), so it is weighed with everything else.
  3. Suspend: we never suspend on one report alone. We suspend on a police or court order, or when 3 or more different people have reported the account within 30 days and the proof has been looked at. A suspended account cannot send messages, send or accept buddy requests, or become anyone's buddy, so it cannot reach anyone. The suspension is tied to its private ID, so reinstalling the app or deleting the account does not lift it. The person sees that the account is suspended and how to appeal to the Grievance Officer.
  4. Preserve: we keep what we lawfully hold about the account (listed in our Privacy Policy, for example its private ID, buddy links, the report and its proof) for as long as the law requires, so it can be given to the authorities.
  5. Report to the authorities: we report child sexual abuse and exploitation as Indian law requires (including the POCSO Act, 2012), to the police and through the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in), and to other bodies such as the US National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) where the law requires or allows. Proof involving a child is passed to the police or cybercrime.gov.in, and kept only as long as needed for that.
  6. Remove: confirmed abuse leads to the account being closed for good.
  7. Cooperate: we answer valid requests from law enforcement quickly.

What we can and cannot see

Messages, photos, videos and voice messages are end-to-end encrypted with the Signal Protocol, and our server deletes each sealed message once it is delivered. This protects people's privacy, and it also means we cannot scan or read messages. That is why reports from users matter, and why we act on the account itself. The only messages we ever see are those a reporter chooses to add as proof.

Please never email us abusive images, and never forward them to anyone, even as evidence: sharing them is itself a crime. Report the account in the app (the sealed proof is the safe way to show us what happened), and report the material directly at cybercrime.gov.in or to the police.
